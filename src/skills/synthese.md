Write a synthesis for someone who will not read the sources.

1. Find out who reads it and what they must decide or do. If the request does not say, assume a colleague who knows the project and say so in one line.
2. Read every source to the end. With a long file, read it in parts with start and end. For this conversation, use <recall> for what is out of view. Do not write from memory.
3. Group by idea, not source by source. Merge what repeats.
4. Write in the user's language, in this order:
   - The conclusion, in two or three sentences.
   - The key points, with the facts and numbers that support them.
   - Decisions taken.
   - Open questions, and what to do next.

Rules:
- Every claim comes from a source. Name it (file, page, message). Add nothing, and never fill a gap: write that the information is missing.
- When sources disagree, give both versions and their source. Do not choose in silence.
- Keep exact figures, names and dates. Mark what is a fact, a decision or an opinion.
- Length: about one tenth of the sources, unless the user gives a length. Omit a section with nothing in it.
- Before you finish, check each number and name against its source.
- If the user asks for a file, write it with <write> and say where it is.
